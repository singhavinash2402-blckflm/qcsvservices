## How To Read This Matrix

- **Confirmed QCSV information** is limited to the service catalog, existing homepage language, and the success stories already present in the project source material.
- **General industry concepts** are planning prompts only. They are not claims about QCSV and must be researched and rewritten in original language.
- **RESEARCH REQUIRED — CLIENT CONFIRMATION MAY BE NEEDED** means the project does not currently contain enough QCSV-specific information to publish a definitive statement.
- No service page should imply a certification, regulatory approval, guaranteed result, named client, quantitative outcome, technology platform, geographic scope, or team capability unless QCSV confirms it.

# Content Governance Rules

## Source Hierarchy

1. QCSV/client-provided material is the primary source for QCSV facts, capabilities, experience, industries, locations, people, outcomes, and success stories.
2. Official regulatory bodies and government sources are used for regulatory and legal concepts. Preferred examples include FDA and the European Commission / EudraLex.
3. Recognized standards and industry organizations are used for general technical and industry concepts. Preferred examples include NIST, ISO, GS1, ISA, SAP, and W3C.
4. Established professional organizations may provide general practice context. PMI is an example for project-management concepts.
5. Competitor websites are never a source for QCSV claims or copied wording.

## Claim Verification

- Every QCSV-specific statement must be traceable to client-provided material or explicitly approved by QCSV.
- General industry information must be labeled or written as context, not as evidence of QCSV capability.
- Success stories may include only the confirmed service, location, and industry listed in this matrix unless QCSV supplies approved details.
- Any new claim about clients, credentials, technologies, industries, locations, outcomes, or delivery scope requires client confirmation.

## Regulatory-Language Rules

- Use measured language such as "support," "assist," "coordinate," "assess," or "provide documentation" when that is what the source supports.
- Do not imply regulatory approval, certification, compliance, audit authority, validation acceptance, or guaranteed readiness.
- Do not name a regulation, standard, framework, or guidance as something QCSV follows unless QCSV confirms it and the current source is reviewed.
- Research summaries must be original and must not reproduce source wording.

## Competitor-Content Prohibition

- Do not copy competitor page structure, wording, claims, case studies, testimonials, diagrams, or differentiators.
- External sources may inform general concepts only; all published wording must be original and appropriate to confirmed QCSV information.

## Approval Requirement For Unsupported QCSV Claims

- A field marked **RESEARCH REQUIRED — CLIENT CONFIRMATION MAY BE NEEDED** is not publication-ready.
- Unsupported claims must remain qualified or be removed until QCSV confirms them in writing.
- No page should be published with invented clients, certifications, awards, quantitative results, regulatory approvals, technologies, team size, geographic coverage, guarantees, compliance claims, or case-study outcomes.

## Review Process Before Publication

1. Confirm the service scope, audience, and intended page purpose with QCSV.
2. Collect approved QCSV facts and map each factual claim to its source.
3. Research only the relevant general industry and regulatory concepts using the source hierarchy.
4. Draft original copy that clearly separates QCSV facts from general context.
5. Run a claim review for unsupported promises, regulatory language, named technologies, industries, locations, and outcomes.
6. Obtain QCSV approval for all service-specific claims and success-story details.
7. Complete editorial, accessibility, link, and technical review before publication.
8. Record material changes and revisit the page when the client-confirmed service scope changes.
