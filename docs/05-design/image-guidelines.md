# QCSV Image Guidelines

**Document type:** Internal design-planning document  
**Purpose:** Define the visual direction and rights-review expectations for future QCSV imagery.  
**Status:** Planning only; no assets have been downloaded, selected, licensed or approved.

## Visual Direction

QCSV imagery should support a modern enterprise identity with regulated-industry credibility. The visual language should connect technology, validation, quality and execution while remaining professional, premium, clean and spacious.

Preferred treatment:

- High-resolution photography or carefully produced original/generated imagery.
- Restrained visual treatment with clear subjects, controlled contrast and useful negative space.
- Compositions that support readable overlay copy without obscuring the subject.
- Consistent photographic art direction across hero, industry and supporting visuals.
- Visuals that feel credible for enterprise decision-makers and regulated or technology-driven organizations.
- Authentic environments and process details rather than generic business symbolism.

Avoid:

- Generic laptop, handshake, office, boardroom or posed-stock imagery.
- Excessive futuristic AI imagery, glowing interfaces or science-fiction styling.
- Copied competitor imagery, layouts or visual concepts.
- Images that imply unsupported QCSV clients, partnerships, locations, certifications or project relationships.
- Fabricated project photography presented as a real QCSV engagement.
- Visuals with visible third-party logos, confidential information, personal data or identifiable customer systems unless rights and permission are verified.
- Low-resolution, heavily compressed, overly dark, excessively staged or difficult-to-crop images.

## Preferred Visual Subjects

Potential subjects include:

- Regulated manufacturing environments.
- Pharmaceutical and laboratory environments.
- Medical-device manufacturing.
- Enterprise technology systems.
- Digital and automation environments.
- Cloud and data infrastructure.
- Engineering, testing and quality processes.
- Professional project collaboration where it communicates real working context without implying an unconfirmed QCSV engagement.

Subjects should be selected as general visual context unless QCSV has approved the image as a depiction of its own people, facilities, customers, systems or work.

## Hero Imagery

Hero imagery should communicate the combination of **technology + regulated environment + systems/quality**. A hero image should:

- Establish the page subject in the first viewport.
- Leave a calm area for readable heading and CTA placement.
- Remain legible in light and dark theme treatments where the product supports both.
- Crop safely across desktop, tablet and mobile aspect ratios.
- Avoid implying a specific client, facility, technology platform, regulatory approval or project outcome.
- Use a real, client-owned, commissioned, licensed, original or generated asset only after rights review.

## Industry Imagery

Industry visuals should be distinctive enough to differentiate Pharmaceuticals, Medical Devices, Life Sciences, Semiconductor and Regulated Manufacturing, while maintaining one photographic or illustrative art direction.

Use subject matter and composition to distinguish industries, not unsupported labels or claims. Industry imagery should communicate context such as laboratories, production systems, engineering environments, instrumentation or manufacturing workflows. It must not imply that QCSV serves a particular named organization or holds an industry certification.

## Success Stories Visual System

Success Stories should preferably use abstract or technical visual treatments rather than fabricated project photography. Suitable directions include:

- Abstract system diagrams created specifically for QCSV.
- Neutral data, workflow or validation motifs.
- Cropped technical details without identifiable customer information.
- Consistent iconographic or geometric treatments tied to service categories.
- Original diagrams or generated artwork that does not imitate competitor materials.

A visual must not imply a case-study challenge, approach, result, customer or technology unless that information is confirmed and the asset is approved for that use.

## Source Categories

Potential source categories include:

- Client-owned or client-commissioned imagery.
- Original QCSV photography or illustration.
- Original generated imagery, with prompt, tool, output and usage record retained.
- Pexels or Unsplash imagery after page-specific license and rights review.
- Other reputable libraries only after their current license terms are reviewed.

Do not use competitor websites as image sources. A source category alone is not evidence that an asset is licensed or approved.

## Rights And Review Rules

Before implementation, record the source URL, creator, license, license URL, download date and local filename in the asset register. Verify current rights terms at the time of use.

Do not mark an asset as **Licensed**, **Approved** or **Ready** until:

1. The actual source has been identified.
2. The applicable license or permission has been reviewed.
3. Usage, modification and attribution requirements are understood.
4. The image has been checked for visible logos, people, private information and implied relationships.
5. The intended page, crop and responsive treatment have been reviewed.
6. QCSV approval is recorded where the asset could imply QCSV-specific people, clients, facilities, technology or work.

## Implementation Notes

- Do not download images during the planning phase represented by this document.
- Do not add imagery to application code until the asset register has a verified source and rights review.
- Preserve original files and record any crop, color, overlay or compositing work.
- Use accessible alternative text based on the actual image content, not an invented business claim.
- Decorative imagery should use an empty alternative-text value when appropriate and must not carry essential meaning alone.
